# Indian Contract Act 1872 — Key Provisions for MSME Contracts (Educational Reference)

**Source**: The Indian Contract Act, 1872 (Act 9 of 1872)
**Status**: In force across India
**Official URL**: https://legislative.gov.in/sites/default/files/A1872-09.pdf
**Last Verified**: 2026

---

## Section 10: What Agreements are Contracts

All agreements are contracts if they are made by:
- the free consent of parties competent to contract,
- for a lawful consideration,
- with a lawful object, and
- are not expressly declared to be void.

**Key elements every valid contract must have**:
1. Offer and acceptance
2. Free consent (no coercion, undue influence, fraud, misrepresentation, mistake)
3. Lawful consideration
4. Capacity to contract (parties must be of sound mind, not minors, not disqualified)
5. Lawful object
6. Not expressly void

## Section 27: Agreement in Restraint of Trade is Void

Every agreement by which any one is restrained from exercising a lawful profession, trade or business of any kind, is to that extent **void**.

**Exception 1**: One who sells the goodwill of a business may agree not to carry on similar business within specified local limits.

**Implication for MSMEs**:
- Post-employment **non-compete clauses are largely unenforceable** in India.
- Indian courts have consistently struck down clauses restricting employees from joining competitors after employment ends.
- However, non-compete clauses **during employment** are valid.
- Non-disclosure agreements (protecting confidential info) are generally enforceable.

**Landmark case reference**: *Niranjan Shankar Golikari v. Century Spinning Mills* (1967) — Supreme Court held that restraints during employment are valid; post-employment restraints generally void.

---

## Section 28: Agreements in Restraint of Legal Proceedings are Void

Every agreement, by which any party thereto is restricted absolutely from enforcing his rights under or in respect of any contract, by the usual legal proceedings in the ordinary tribunals, or which limits the time within which he may thus enforce his rights, is void.

**Exception**: Arbitration agreements are valid.

**Red flag for MSMEs**: Watch out for clauses that:
- Bar all legal recourse
- Set unreasonably short time limits for raising disputes
- Force jurisdiction in distant cities making litigation impractical

## Section 73: Compensation for Loss or Damage Caused by Breach of Contract

When a contract has been broken, the party who suffers by such breach is entitled to receive compensation for any loss or damage which:
- Naturally arose in the usual course of things from such breach, OR
- Which the parties knew, when they made the contract, was likely to result from the breach.

**Important**: Compensation is NOT to be given for any **remote and indirect loss** sustained by reason of the breach.

**Implication for MSMEs**: Indemnity clauses that demand "all losses, indirect, consequential, special, punitive damages" are often unenforceable in India because of Section 73. However, having such clauses still creates negotiation friction during disputes.

---

## Section 74: Liquidated Damages

When a contract has been broken, and a sum is named in the contract as the amount to be paid in case of such breach, the party complaining of the breach is entitled to receive from the party who has broken the contract reasonable compensation **not exceeding** the amount so named.

**Key principle**: Even if the contract names ₹10 lakhs as penalty, Indian courts will only award what is "reasonable compensation" based on actual proven loss.

## Common Red Flags in MSME Contracts

### 1. Unilateral Termination Clauses
**Watch for language like**: "The Buyer may terminate this Agreement at any time without cause by giving 30 days notice."
**Why it's a red flag**: One-sided termination rights give the larger party complete control.
**Mitigation**: Push for mutual termination rights with the same notice period.

### 2. Unlimited Liability
**Watch for**: Absence of any liability cap, or a very high cap like "Supplier shall be liable for all direct, indirect, consequential, punitive, special damages without limit."
**Why it's a red flag**: Could result in catastrophic financial exposure for the smaller party.
**Mitigation**: Negotiate liability cap of "fees paid in last 12 months" or 1x contract value.

### 3. Broad Indemnity
**Watch for**: "Supplier shall indemnify Buyer against ALL claims, losses, damages, including third-party claims."
**Why it's a red flag**: One-sided risk shifting.
**Mitigation**: Mutual indemnity, only for direct losses arising from negligence/breach.

### 4. Payment Terms Exceeding 45 Days
**Watch for**: Payment terms of 60, 90, or 120 days.
**Why it's a red flag**: Violates Section 15 of MSMED Act if you're an MSME supplier.
**Mitigation**: Either reduce to 45 days OR ensure the contract acknowledges the supplier is not registered as MSME.

### 5. Jurisdiction in Distant Cities
**Watch for**: "Exclusive jurisdiction of courts in [Mumbai/Bangalore/Delhi]" when neither party operates there.
**Why it's a red flag**: Makes legal recourse impractical and expensive.
**Mitigation**: Either neutral arbitration seat or convenient jurisdiction for both parties.

### 6. Auto-Renewal Without Notification
**Watch for**: "This Agreement shall automatically renew for successive periods of one year unless either party gives 90 days written notice prior to expiry."
**Why it's a red flag**: Easy to forget renewal deadline; locks you into unwanted continuation.
**Mitigation**: Require affirmative renewal (i.e., must be re-signed), or shorter renewal periods.

### 7. Broad IP Assignment
**Watch for**: "All intellectual property, including pre-existing IP, developed during the engagement shall be the property of the Buyer."
**Why it's a red flag**: You could lose rights to your existing tools, methodologies.
**Mitigation**: Carve out pre-existing IP; assign only IP specifically created for the project.

### 8. Confidentiality Without Time Limit
**Watch for**: "Confidentiality obligations shall survive indefinitely."
**Why it's a red flag**: Forever obligations are impractical and often unenforceable.
**Mitigation**: 3-5 year confidentiality period post-termination.

### 9. Penalty Clauses Without Reciprocity
**Watch for**: "Supplier shall pay ₹50,000 per day for delays, but no penalty on Buyer for delayed payments."
**Why it's a red flag**: One-sided punishment.
**Mitigation**: Mutual penalties OR remove penalties entirely (Section 74 limits enforceability anyway).

### 10. Force Majeure That's One-Sided
**Watch for**: Force majeure protection only for one party.
**Why it's a red flag**: Post-COVID, force majeure has become critical.
**Mitigation**: Mutual force majeure with clear definitions including pandemics, lockdowns.

## Standard Protections That SHOULD Be Present

Every well-drafted contract should have:

1. **Limitation of Liability**: Cap on damages
2. **Force Majeure**: Mutual, includes natural disasters, pandemics, government actions
3. **Dispute Resolution**: Specifies arbitration (preferred) or court process
4. **Governing Law**: Indian law, specific state
5. **Notices**: How parties communicate officially
6. **Severability**: If one clause is invalid, rest of contract survives
7. **Entire Agreement**: Supersedes prior discussions
8. **Amendment Procedure**: Changes only in writing, signed by both parties
9. **Counterparts**: Can be signed in multiple copies
10. **Cure Period**: Time to fix a breach before termination

## Stamp Duty Requirements

Most contracts in India require stamp paper. Common rates (varies by state, check your state's Stamp Act):

| Contract Type | Stamp Duty (Approximate) |
|---|---|
| Service Agreement | ₹100-500 |
| Lease (less than 1 year) | 0.5-1% of total rent |
| Lease (more than 11 months) | Requires registration too |
| Loan Agreement | 0.1-0.5% of loan amount |
| Employment Agreement | ₹100 |
| Power of Attorney | ₹100-500 |

**Note**: Unstamped or under-stamped documents may not be admissible as evidence in court.
