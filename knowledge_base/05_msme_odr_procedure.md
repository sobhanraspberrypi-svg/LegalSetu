# MSME ODR Portal — Procedural Guide (Educational Reference)

**Source**: Ministry of Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises, Government of India
**Official Portal**: https://msmeodr.gov.in
**Old Portal (Samadhaan)**: https://samadhaan.msme.gov.in (now superseded by ODR Portal)
**Last Verified**: 2026

---

## What is the MSME ODR Portal?

The MSME Online Dispute Resolution (ODR) Portal is an official government platform launched in 2025 to enable Micro and Small Enterprises (MSEs) to recover delayed payments from buyers through a digital, time-bound dispute resolution mechanism.

It replaces the older MSME Samadhaan portal for all new delayed payment cases.

---

## Who Can File?

Eligibility:
1. Must be a Micro or Small Enterprise (Medium enterprises are NOT covered)
2. Must have Udyam Registration **before** the invoice/transaction date
3. Must have supplied goods or services to a buyer
4. Payment must be overdue beyond the agreed period (or 45 days, whichever is earlier)

---

## What Cases Cannot Be Filed?

- Disputes initiated by buyer against supplier (one-way protection only)
- Disputes involving medium enterprises as the supplier
- Disputes where Udyam registration was obtained AFTER the invoice date
- Disputes purely about quality of goods (without payment recovery angle)

## Step-by-Step Filing Process

### Step 1: Pre-Filing Preparation

Before going to the portal, collect:
- [ ] Udyam Registration Certificate
- [ ] Purchase Order (if any)
- [ ] Tax Invoice(s)
- [ ] Proof of Delivery (LR, e-way bill, delivery challan)
- [ ] Acknowledgement of receipt from buyer
- [ ] Email/written communications about payment
- [ ] Bank statement showing non-receipt
- [ ] Any contract or agreement
- [ ] Demand notice already sent (recommended)

### Step 2: Portal Registration

1. Visit https://msmeodr.gov.in
2. Click "Register / Login"
3. Use your Udyam Registration Number
4. Verify mobile/email OTP
5. Set up profile

### Step 3: File New Case

1. Login to your dashboard
2. Click "File New Case"
3. Select "Delayed Payment"
4. Enter buyer details:
   - Name and address
   - GSTIN (if available)
   - Type of entity (Pvt Ltd, LLP, Proprietorship, etc.)
   - Contact details
5. Enter invoice details:
   - Invoice number, date, amount
   - Agreed payment terms
   - Date of acceptance (if different)
6. Upload all documents
7. Calculate interest (portal does this automatically)
8. Review and submit

### Step 4: Filing Fee

Nominal filing fee applies (subject to revision; check current rates on portal).

### Step 5: Phase 1 — Automated Negotiation

- Buyer is notified
- 15-30 days for direct settlement
- Portal facilitates communication
- If settled: Case closed with mutually agreed terms
- If not settled: Moves to Phase 2

### Step 6: Phase 2 — Conciliation

- Referred to MSEFC (Micro and Small Enterprises Facilitation Council)
- Council may conduct conciliation itself or refer to ADR institution
- Provisions of Sections 65-81 of Arbitration & Conciliation Act 1996 apply
- Timeline: Typically 60-90 days
- If settled: Settlement is binding
- If not settled: Moves to Phase 3

### Step 7: Phase 3 — Arbitration

- Council either conducts arbitration or refers to ADR institution
- Provisions of Arbitration & Conciliation Act 1996 apply
- Timeline: 90 days from arbitration commencement (extendable)
- Award is binding on both parties

### Step 8: Post-Award

If buyer wishes to challenge the award:
- Must file under Section 34 of Arbitration & Conciliation Act 1996
- **Mandatory pre-deposit of 75% of award amount** under Section 18(4) MSMED Act
- This is a strong deterrent — many awards are settled rather than challenged

## Key Statutory Provisions

### Section 16 of MSMED Act (Interest)

- Interest at **3 times the bank rate notified by RBI**
- **Compounded monthly**
- Current approximate rate: 19.5% per annum (verify RBI bank rate)

### Section 15 of MSMED Act (Payment Period)

- Payment as agreed in writing, OR
- Where no written agreement: before the appointed day
- Maximum permissible period: **45 days** from acceptance/deemed acceptance

### Section 17 of MSMED Act (Recovery)

The buyer is liable to pay amount with interest as per Section 16.

### Section 18 of MSMED Act (Dispute Resolution)

- Reference to MSEFC
- Council to conduct conciliation, then arbitration
- Application of Arbitration & Conciliation Act 1996

### Section 23 of MSMED Act

- Interest paid by buyer is NOT allowable as deduction under Income Tax Act
- Strong deterrent for buyers to pay on time

## Common Buyer Defences (and Council's Stance)

### Defence: "Quality of goods was poor"
- Council's view: Buyer should have raised quality objection within reasonable time of delivery
- Cannot withhold payment indefinitely on this ground
- Council may adjust amount if quality defence has merit, but interest continues

### Defence: "Goods not yet accepted"
- Section 2(b) defines "day of acceptance" as either day of delivery or day of deemed acceptance (15 days from delivery if no objection)
- Buyer cannot indefinitely delay "acceptance"

### Defence: "Different agreement on payment terms"
- Even if longer than 45 days agreed in writing, Section 15 caps at 45 days
- Any longer agreement is unenforceable to that extent

### Defence: "Supplier was not MSE at time of supply"
- Burden on supplier to prove Udyam registration was valid at time of supply
- Submit Udyam certificate with registration date

## Realistic Timeline Expectations

| Phase | Duration |
|---|---|
| Filing | 1-3 days |
| Buyer notification | 7 days |
| Phase 1 (Auto-negotiation) | 15-30 days |
| Phase 2 (Conciliation) | 60-90 days |
| Phase 3 (Arbitration) | 90-180 days |
| **Total realistic timeline** | **4-9 months for an award** |

This is significantly faster than civil court litigation (typically 5-10+ years).
